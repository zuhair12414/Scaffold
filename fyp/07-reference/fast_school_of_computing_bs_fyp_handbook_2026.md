## 1. Glossary
- **FYP:** BS Final Year Project
- **Student:** A student registered for an FYP at NUCES
- **FYP Group:** A group of students formed as a team to work on an FYP
- **Faculty:** Permanent Computing faculty at NUCES
- **Supervisor:** A person responsible for the supervision of an FYP Group. S/he is a NUCES Faculty member.
- **Faculty Panel:** A subset of Faculty responsible for the evaluation of an FYP. External experts of the relevant area can also be included.
- **Panel Head:** A member of Faculty Panel responsible for the supervision of the panel examination.
- **FYP Committee:** A committee of Faculty responsible for executing the FYP process.
- **FYP Coordinators:** Responsible for managing FYP registrations, evaluations, and results.

## 2. The Role of FYP Committee
For smooth execution of FYP process, the FYP committee carries out all the underlying supporting activities and provides the basic working platform for the execution of the FYPs.

### Stages of FYP Committee Lifecycle
1. **Step 1: Registration of FYP-1 Groups**
   - Eligibility check: Groups must consist of 2 or 3 members (3 preferred). No solo projects or groups >3 allowed.
   - Required information:
     i. Title and brief scope description (1 paragraph description of 8–10 key features).  
     ii. UN SDG(s) targeted.  
     iii. Supervisor name and group member details (names, roll numbers, CGPAs).  
     iv. Category of project and technologies to be used.  
     v. Evidence of Complex Computing Problem (CCP).  
   - Submission is via online form by end of Week 2. Flex course registration is a mandatory prerequisite.
2. **Step 2: FYP Proposal Defense**
   - Form faculty panels, schedule presentations, process results, distribute feedback, and handle re-evaluations.
3. **Step 3: FYP-1 Mid Evaluation**
   - Collect soft reports in Week 8; share evaluation forms with supervisors for iterative assessment.
4. **Step 4: FYP Poster Preparation and Evaluation**
   - Verify format, coordinate printing and public display around Week 13–15, and compile marks.
5. **Step 5: Final FYP-1 Evaluation**
   - Organize panel presentations and supervisor report grading (45% implementation expectation). Apply anomaly removal mechanisms.
6. **Step 6: Preparation of FYP-1 Grade**
   - Compile grades (50% pass requirement). Differentiate individual marks if significant performance disparity is noted.
7. **Step 7: Execution of FYP-2**
   - Center execution around the Job Fair (formerly Open-House) with industry evaluators.
8. **Step 8-a & 8-b: Pre-Job Fair Evaluation & Re-Evaluation**
   - Week 8 gate check to qualify projects for the Job Fair. Missing the fair results in up to a 30% penalty on FYP-2.
9. **Step 9: Managing the Job Fair**
   - Held around Weeks 12–14 in coordination with industrial liaison.
10. **Step 10: Final FYP-2 Evaluation**
    - Supervisor-led assessment at Week 15 along with anti-plagiarism vetting.
11. **Step 11: Final Deliverables and Release of Grades**
    - Final deliverables verification (code, report, manual, demo) prior to grade release.

### Scope Boundaries of the FYP Committee
- **Not Covered:** Routine monitoring/mentoring (supervisor duty); attendance tracking (supervisor duty); physical computing/lab infrastructure maintenance (IT & lab management duty).

## 3. The Role of a Supervisor
1. Defining the scope of the project.
2. Guiding the students about technical issues.
3. Monitoring weekly progress and assigning tasks via prescribed monitoring forms.
4. Enforcing adherence to FYP Committee deadlines.
5. Reporting dysfunctional teams; requiring at least one formal meeting per week. Students missing meetings can be debarred with an 'F'.
6. Instilling engineering ethics, teamwork, and adherence to an iterative development lifecycle.

## 5. The Process

### 5.1 An Agile and Iterative FYP Process
Transition away from heavy theoretical waterfall documentation with zero code in FYP-1. Students must complete requirements, analysis, design, and functional implementation iteratively across both semesters.

### 5.2 FYP Iteration
- Iterations represent concrete units of deliverable functionality.
- For development projects, students pick specific use cases, design them, and deliver a working product demo per iteration.
- **FYP-1 Target:** Complete at least 2 iterations representing approximately **45% of total project implementation**.
- **FYP-2 Target:** Complete remaining iterations, full system integration, and deployment.

#### Table 1a: FYP-1 Evaluations
| S. # | Deliverable / Evaluation | Week # | Deliverable / Presentation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Proposal Defense | 4 | Proposal document and presentation with **PoC-Lite** |
| 2 | Mid Evaluation | 8 | **FYP Report (v1.0):** Project Vision, Literature/Product Research, Use Cases, SSD, SRS, Test Plan, Software Dev Plan, Wireframes, DEI & Ethical/Legal Statements.<br>**Iteration-1 Design & Code:** Structural Design (Class/Component/Layer/Structure chart), Behavioral Design (DFD, Activity, State Machine, Sequence), DB Schema/ERD, Code with Unit Tests, CI/CD setup, Deployment diagram, Working Code + Demo. |
| 3 | Poster Submission | 13 | Submission of Poster to the FYP Committee |
| 4 | Poster Evaluation | 15 | Displayed in public area and evaluated (can be done during final evaluation) |
| 5 | Final Evaluation | 15 | **FYP Report (v2.0)** with corrections, subsequent iterations, working code demonstration (~45% implemented), test suites. |

#### Table 1b: FYP-2 Evaluations
| S. # | Deliverable / Evaluation | Week # | Deliverable / Presentation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-Job Fair Evaluation | 8 | Updated posters, demonstration readiness, updated report submission. |
| 2 | FYP Pre-Job Fair (Re-eval) | 9 | Re-evaluation of projects failing the Week 8 gate. |
| 3 | Job Fair | 12–14 | Public industry showcase; CVs, project profiles, graduate directory assets. |
| 4 | Final FYP-2 Evaluation | 15 | Complete signed FYP report, package/deployment diagrams, repo links, app store releases (if mobile), configuration/tool manual. |
| 5 | FYP-2 Final Deliverables | 17 | Final soft copy report, presentation slides, full source code, manual, demo executables. |

### FYP-1 Process Breakdown
- Registration open to students finishing degree next semester. Up to 1 course relaxation allowed by Head of Computing (2nd course by Campus Director).
- External/industry projects require a permanent internal faculty supervisor and a 3-page formal proposal.
- Week 4 Proposal Defense decisions: Approved, Approved with Minor Modifications, Approved with Major Modifications (No Re-defense), Re-defense Required, or Rejected.
- Hardware/software purchase requests must be justified via supervisor application (granted on a case-by-case basis).
- Number of use cases does not indicate quality; quality lies in technical depth, algorithmic challenge, and implementation evidence.

### FYP-2 Process Breakdown
- Open only to students passing FYP-1.
- Pre-Job Fair evaluation in Week 8 determines eligibility for the industry exhibition.
- Week 15 final demo with supervisor; Week 17 final handover of software, report, and documentation.
- Mandatory deliverables: Report, Presentation, Reference Materials, Source Code, and Demonstrable Executable/User Manual.

## 6. Evaluation & Grading

### Evaluators' Weights (FYP-1 & FYP-2)
| Evaluation Component | Weight Range |
|---|---|
| Supervisor(s) | 35% – 60% |
| Faculty Panel (treated as single collective examiner) | 40% – 65% |

### Documentation Breakdown (Finals Only)
| Component | FYP-1 Final (50 Marks) | FYP-2 Final (50 Marks) |
|---|---|---|
| Live Demo + Technical Defense | 42.5 Marks (85%) | 42.5 Marks (85%) |
| Written Report / Documentation | 7.5 Marks (15%) | 7.5 Marks (15%) |

### Miscellaneous Administrative Rules
1. **Proposal Failure:** Requires resubmission and re-defense (same or new supervisor).
2. **Supervisor Changes:** Requires written consent of the existing supervisor unless switching ideas completely with coordinator approval.
3. **Continuous Registration:** FYP-1 and FYP-2 must be registered in consecutive semesters (no freezing in between).
4. **Failure ('F') in FYP-1:** Must re-register FYP-1.
5. **Failure ('F') in FYP-2:** Must re-register FYP-2 on the same project idea; changing ideas at this stage requires retaking FYP-1 unless formally exempted by HoD.
6. **Supervisor Departure:** FYP Committee assigns a replacement faculty supervisor.
7. **Late Submission / Missing Meetings:** Unapproved late report submissions or chronic absenteeism can lead to debarment and an automatic 'F' grade.

## 7. Guidelines for the Preparation of FYP Poster
- **Dimensions:** $22.5" \times 34.5"$ portrait orientation.
- **Visual Design:** High readability from 1 meter distance. Avoid dense blocks of text; use bullet points, system diagrams, UI screen captures, and charts.
- **Layout Scheme:**
  - **Top-Left:** Project title, student names, supervisor name(s).
  - **Top-Right:** Project Goals & Objectives.
  - **Bottom:** Project Plan, current progress, tentative final deliverables.
- **Submission Formats:** Editable source file (Photoshop/Corel Draw) along with a high-resolution JPEG.

## 8. Guidelines for FYP Report Format

### Formatting Specifications
- **Paper Size:** Standard A4 ($8.27" \times 11.69"$), 90 gsm.
- **Margins:** Top: $1.5"$, Bottom: $1.0"$, Left: $2.0"$, Right: $1.0"$.
- **Typography:**
  - Regular Text: Times New Roman, 11 pt, 1.5 line spacing, 6 pt paragraph after-space.
  - Title: Times New Roman, 26 pt Bold.
  - Subtitle: Times New Roman, 20 pt Bold.
  - Heading 1: Arial, 16 pt Bold.
  - Heading 2: Arial, 14 pt Bold.
  - Heading 3: Arial, 13 pt Bold Italic.
- **Indentation:** Block quotations of 4+ lines indented 5 spaces from left margin.
- **Pagination:** Lower-case Roman numerals ($i, ii, iii...$) for preliminary pages up to introduction. Arabic numerals ($1, 2, 3...$) starting from introduction onwards, placed at top-right corner.
- **Headers:** Odd pages show the Report Title; even pages show Chapter/Section Title. No headers on opening chapter pages.

### Mandatory Declarations
- **Anti-Plagiarism Declaration:** Formal signed warranty certifying original work, correct citations, and acceptance of disciplinary penalties if violated.
- **Authors' Declaration:** Signed statement confirming the work has not been previously submitted to any other institution.

## 9. FYP Report Contents
A standard report structure must contain:
1. **Title Page, Declarations, Abstract** (50–125 words), Acknowledgements, Executive Summary (1–2 pages), Table of Contents.
2. **Introduction:** Background, problem context, justification of value.
3. **Research on Existing Ideas / Products:** Literature survey, competitor matrix, state-of-the-art gap.
4. **Project Vision:** Problem Statement, Business Opportunity, Objectives, Scope, Constraints, Stakeholder Summary, High-Level Goals, DEI & Ethical/Legal Statements.
5. **Software Requirement Specifications (SRS):** Feature List, Functional Requirements (FRs), Non-Functional Requirements (NFRs).
6. **Iteration Plan:** Breakdown across semesters and sprints.
7. **Iteration 1 Artifacts:** Design (Class, Component, Layer, DFD, Sequence/Activity, ERD), Test Plan, CI/CD, Deployment model.
8. **Iteration 2 & Subsequent Artifacts:** System evolution, integration diagrams, end-to-end data flows.
9. **Implementation Details:** Non-trivial algorithmic logic, data processing pipelines, mathematical formulations.
10. **User Manual & Operational Guides.**
11. **References & Appendices.**

### Stage 0: Proposal Defense (FYP-1, Week 4)
- **Evaluator:** Panel + Supervisor | **Duration:** 18–20 min | **Weight:** 30 Marks
- **Core Intent:** Scoping and feasibility gate. Confirms the problem qualifies as a Complex Computing Problem (CCP), surveys the landscape honestly, and checks technical viability.
- **Evaluator's Key Question:** *"Is this problem real, is this approach viable, and is this group capable of executing it over two semesters?"*
- **What Good Looks Like:**
  - Clear gap between current solutions and contribution; requires deep computing knowledge.
  - Justified architecture and technology choices.
  - Demonstrated **POC-lite** addressing the riskiest assumption (API integration, baseline run, or data pipeline—not just a UI mockup).
  - Traceable, balanced member work allocation.
  - Transparent disclosure of AI/pre-trained models and their intended role.

---

### Stage 1: FYP-1 Mid (FYP-1, Week 8)
- **Evaluator:** Supervisor only | **Duration:** 15 min | **Weight:** 20 Marks
- **Core Intent:** Formative supervisory checkpoint. Evaluates evolving design reasoning, implementation momentum, and progress against proposal action items.
- **Evaluator's Key Question:** *"Is the group on a trajectory that will produce something defensible and substantively understood by the end of FYP-1, and what must change in the remaining weeks to ensure that?"*
- **What Good Looks Like:**
  - Working live module implementing core logic beyond the POC-lite.
  - Evolved design artifacts reflecting real engineering choices.
  - Codebase walkthrough showing genuine code comprehension (no blind GenAI copy-pasting).
  - Honest milestone tracking with explicit recovery plans for delays.
  - Testing demonstrated beyond happy paths (handling failure/edge conditions).

---

### Stage 2: FYP-1 Final (FYP-1, Final Week)
- **Evaluator:** Panel (Demo) + Supervisor/Examiner (Report) | **Duration:** 15 min + report | **Weight:** 50 Marks (42.5 Demo + 7.5 Report)
- **Core Intent:** Integration readiness gate. Confirms that ~45% of scope is modular, readable, buildable, and structurally sound.
- **Evaluator's Key Question:** *"Is the work completed in FYP-1 of sufficient quality, comprehension, and structural integrity to support full integration and evaluation in FYP-2?"*
- **What Good Looks Like:**
  - Modular, reproducible code running outside a single developer laptop.
  - Individual mastery: each student explains design trade-offs and edge cases for their assigned module.
  - Testing executed and documented; failure paths explored.
  - Written report precisely matches the demonstrated reality.
  - Concrete FYP-2 evaluation plan defined (baselines, datasets, metrics).

---

### Stage 3: FYP-2 Mid / Pre-Job Fair (FYP-2, Week 8)
- **Evaluator:** Panel + Supervisor | **Duration:** 12–15 min | **Weight:** 50 Marks
- **Core Intent:** Dual academic progress assessment and public readiness gate for Job Fair participation.
- **Evaluator's Key Question:** *"Has the group produced an integrated, working system that demonstrates real engineering ownership and is ready to be presented publicly to industry—and if not, what must change before the job fair?"*
- **What Good Looks Like:**
  - End-to-end integrated system across all major modules.
  - System handles realistic user scenarios and degrades gracefully under failure.
  - Clear communication suitable for both academic evaluators and industry recruiters.
  - CCP resolution is observable directly in the running system.
  - Integration testing and preliminary baseline comparisons presented.
  - Complete GenAI and external tool disclosure defended.

---

### Stage 4: FYP-2 Final (FYP-2, Final Week)
- **Evaluator:** Supervisor only | **Duration:** 10–12 min + report | **Weight:** 50 Marks (42.5 Demo + 7.5 Report)
- **Core Intent:** Professional closure, handover, and reflective contribution evaluation.
- **Evaluator's Key Question:** *"Can each student give an honest, substantiated account of what the system does, what it does not do, what they personally built and understood, and what they would do differently—and does the final report reflect that account faithfully?"*
- **What Good Looks Like:**
  - Fully functioning deployed system without regressions from Mid-2.
  - Faithful final report tracing requirements through design, code, and validation.
  - Mature individual reflection: personal ownership, tool contribution vs. self-authored logic, and limitations.
  - Honest analysis of system boundaries, unhandled edge cases, and future work.

### 2. Common Rules for All Forms

#### 2.1 Scoring Scale
| Score Band | Description |
|---|---|
| **9–10** | **Strong evidence:** Deep understanding, justified decisions, working artifacts, validation, confident defense. |
| **7–8** | **Good evidence:** Minor gaps, but clear engineering reasoning and personal ownership. |
| **5–6** | **Basic evidence:** Work exists, but explanation, validation depth, or technical quality is limited. |
| **3–4** | **Weak evidence:** Superficial work, limited understanding, weak traceability, incomplete demo. |
| **0–2** | **No meaningful evidence:** Non-working, hidden dependencies, or student unable to explain the code. |

#### 2.2 Score Caps and Automatic Actions
- **No live system at demo stage:** Implementation marks capped at 40% maximum.
- **Core feature is an uncustomized third-party API/template:** Core implementation marks capped at 50%; panel may mandate rescoping.
- **Undisclosed GenAI / API / pre-trained model usage:** Responsible tool-use criterion = 0; refer to disciplinary committee for academic integrity violation.
- **Student cannot explain claimed module:** Apply individual multiplier reduction; group mark remains intact.
- **Polished diagrams/reports without design rationale:** Design marks capped at 50%.
- **Git commit spamming without defense comprehension:** Commit volume alone receives zero contribution credit.

#### 2.3 Responsible External Tool / API / GenAI Use Rubric
- **Level 1 (Supportive Use):** Formatting, boilerplate, syntax check, grammar. *Permitted without special defense.*
- **Level 2 (Assisted Development):** AI generated code/tests/diagrams, but students reviewed, modified, benchmarked, and defend every line. *Permitted with full credit.*
- **Level 3 (Heavy Core Assistance):** External API/model performs heavy computation; students build custom integration, fine-tuning, fallbacks, and validation pipelines. *Conditional full credit based on rigor of added value.*
- **Level 4 (Substituted Work):** Third-party tool/template solves the project core; usage is obfuscated or unexplainable. *Unacceptable: score caps, project rejection, or integrity proceedings.*

## Appendix C: FYP Idea Selection and Proposal Readiness Guide

### Core Standard
Every FYP, regardless of stream or supervisor, must exhibit **six connected pillars**:
1. Research-informed problem understanding.
2. Clearly justified Complex Computing Problem (CCP) or activity.
3. Coherent designed and implemented system running in a realistic deployment environment.
4. Systematic testing and empirical evaluation.
5. Standardized engineering documentation set (the final report).
6. Traceable team and verifiable individual technical ownership.

---

### C.1 What kind of FYP should you select?

#### C.1.1 Two Entry Points, One Standard
- **Stream A (Research-Derived):** Starts from peer-reviewed papers, benchmarks, open algorithms, or published datasets.
  - *Primary Risk:* Can it be reproduced and engineered into a deployable system within local compute/time limits?
  - *Common Failure:* Running someone else's Jupyter notebook without building an operable application.
- **Stream B (Application-Derived):** Starts from an observed real-world failure, unmet stakeholder need, or broken workflow.
  - *Primary Risk:* Is the problem authentic, and can data/stakeholder access be secured?
  - *Common Failure:* Building a slick application that lacks computational depth (CRUD trap).

| Dimension | Stream A (Research-Derived) | Stream B (Application-Derived) |
|---|---|---|
| **Starting Point** | Peer-reviewed papers, benchmarks, datasets | Observed workflow failure, manual process |
| **Source of Gap** | Stated limitations, untested operational settings | Inadequate existing products, broken workflows |
| **Early Risk** | Compute constraints, algorithm reproducibility | Stakeholder engagement, data access |
| **Typical Output** | Novel pipeline, adapted method, benchmark system | Constrained domain architecture, specialized system |
| **Panel Focus** | *"Can you explain the method without looking at the code?"* | *"Where is the computational complexity beyond basic UI/DB?"* |

#### C.1.2 Working, Deployable System
- Must run outside the local development environment (cloud, container, mobile device, edge hardware).
- Requires deliberate handling of environment secrets, credentials, and seeds.
- Graceful degradation and fallback paths for unhandled errors.
- Handover documentation so external developers can run and build the codebase.

#### C.1.3 SDG or Thematic Alignment
- Identify one relevant UN Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) or thematic area (Health, Education, Smart Cities, Accessibility).
- *Warning:* SDG alignment provides contextual relevance only; it cannot compensate for a weak technical core or lack of CCP.

---

### C.2 Research and Development is Embedded in Every FYP
- FYP research means evidence-informed engineering: mapping competitor products, understanding architectural trade-offs, and defining baselines.
- Two encouraged high-ambition archetypes:
  1. Engineering a state-of-the-art research method into an operable, production-ready system under real latency/memory constraints.
  2. Improving upon published algorithmic gaps and evaluating the new method against the original baseline.

---

### C.3 Understanding a Complex Computing Problem (CCP)

#### The 9 Seoul Accord CCP Characteristics
1. **Conflicting Requirements:** Competing technical demands (e.g., high accuracy vs. edge latency; privacy vs. personalization).
2. **No Obvious Solution / In-depth Analysis:** Cannot be solved by copying standard templates; requires comparative algorithmic analysis.
3. **Depth of Knowledge:** Demands fundamentals of advanced computing disciplines (e.g., algorithms, systems, distributed networking, ML theory).
4. **Unfamiliar Issues:** Non-routine challenges emerging from real problem constraints.
5. **Beyond Standard Practice:** Exceeds standard boilerplate patterns or library tutorials.
6. **Diverse Stakeholders:** Multiple conflicting roles, access tiers, or administrative authorities.
7. **Significant Consequences:** System failure incurs safety, monetary, operational, or legal hazards.
8. **Interdependence:** Interconnected subsystems where changes propagate unpredictably across modules.
9. **Ill-defined Requirements or Cause:** Core root cause or requirement structure is ambiguous and requires empirical discovery.

*Rule:* Complexity must arise naturally from problem constraints, not from artificially stacking frameworks.

---

### C.4 Contribution: What Value Will Your FYP Add?
- **Contribution Formula:**
  > *"Existing approaches perform X, but remain weak under Y condition. We will contribute Z through A, and evaluate it using B."*
- **Recognized Contribution Types:**
  - *Problem/Requirement Contribution:* Formalizing neglected workflows under severe domain constraints.
  - *Method/Algorithm Contribution:* Adapting or optimizing techniques for novel operational settings.
  - *System/Architecture Contribution:* Low-latency, edge, offline-first, or distributed fault-tolerant design.
  - *Data/Resource Contribution:* Curated domain benchmarks, verified datasets, and annotation pipelines.
  - *Human/Workflow Contribution:* Human-in-the-loop decision-support, accessibility, explainable interfaces.
  - *Evaluation Contribution:* Systematic empirical comparisons, stress testing, failure mode analysis.

---

### C.5 Scope for the Approved Group Across Two Semesters
- A single coherent system with modular ownership, not three independent micro-projects.
- **Diagnostic Scope Test:** If any member were removed, does a critical technical subsystem disappear? If not, the scope distribution is defective.

#### Five Gates Lifecycle
1. **Gate 1 (Proposal Defense, W4):** Scope approved, POC-lite demonstrated, initial RAID register.
2. **Gates 2 & 3 (FYP-1 Mid, W8):** SRS baselined, Software Design baselined, live prototype module.
3. **Exit Gate (FYP-1 Final, W15):** ~45% Core build accepted, end-to-end integration path verified.
4. **Gate 4 (FYP-2 Mid, W8):** Release readiness, integrated system testing complete, Job Fair clearance.
5. **Gate 5 (FYP-2 Final, W15):** Deployed system acceptance, complete evaluation, final handover.

#### Client Role Definition
- *Industry Project:* Industry partner acts as client; supervisor acts as academic gatekeeper.
- *Supervisor Project:* Supervisor acts as both client and academic mentor.
- *Group-Originated Project:* Group writes scope on behalf of a consulted stakeholder; supervisor approves.

---

### C.6 Can This Idea Be Specified?
An idea is ready only if it can be specified across six concrete checks:
1. State 5 functional requirements in the formal syntax: `"The system shall <action> for <actor>."`
2. State at least one measurable non-functional requirement (e.g., `"Sub-200ms latency at 95th percentile under 50 concurrent requests"`).
3. Explicitly identify system actors and shared data structures.
4. Formulate testable acceptance criteria for the hardest module.
5. Sketch a one-page component/interface architecture.
6. Identify the most volatile, risk-prone subsystem.

---

### C.7 Will This Idea Survive Generative AI?
- **The Generative AI Resilience Test:** If a single student using modern AI tools can generate the entire functional application in a weekend, the idea is fundamentally inadequate for a capstone project.
- Capstone difficulty resides in: data curation, messy operational constraints, hardware boundaries, complex edge cases, trade-off optimization, and empirical evaluation.

---

### C.8 AI-Assisted Development in an FYP

```
Maturity Spectrum:
[Level 1: Traditional] ──────► [Level 2: AI-Assisted] ──────► [Level 3: AI-DLC / Agentic]
(100% Human Executed)         (Human Judgement & Execution;   (AI Leads Execution;
                               AI Supports Isolated Tasks)     Human Validates/Approves)
                                           ▲
                                           │
                                    *FYP SITS HERE*
```

#### Why FYPs Sit at Level 2 (AI-Assisted)
- **Accreditation Mandate:** Seoul Accord GA4 requires students to design and evaluate solutions. If the AI designs the solution, the graduate attribute cannot be attained.
- **Competence Precedes Judgment:** Validating generated code requires senior engineering competence built by writing and debugging code manually first.

#### The Ownership Boundary
- **Students Must Always Own:** Problem formulation, CCP identification, architecture, trade-off analysis, baseline evaluation, failure mode analysis, and technical defense.
- **AI May Assist With (with disclosure):** Scaffolding, boilerplate, syntax troubleshooting, refactoring, test suite generation, and API documentation discovery.
- **Golden Rule:** *"Authorship of keystrokes is not the test; comprehension, justification, and the ability to modify the codebase live under panel interrogation are."*

---

### C.9 A Disciplined Idea-Discovery Process
1. Explore real problem spaces and interview stakeholders.
2. Map the existing solution landscape (products, research, open-source).
3. Verify the remaining gap with evidence.
4. Formulate the CCP using Seoul Accord characteristics.
5. Define the precise technical contribution.
6. Benchmark and compare candidate approaches.
7. Build and execute a focused proposal POC / technical spike.
8. Define empirical metrics and baseline success criteria.
9. Divide work into modular responsibilities; address DEI, legal, and ethical constraints.
10. Stress-test assumptions and failure conditions.
11. Connect contextual SDG alignment.

### C.13 Plan Evaluation Before Approval
- Projects must distinguish between **Testing** (verification against spec), **Evaluation** (empirical benchmarking vs. baselines), and **User Acceptance** (validation by real stakeholders).

#### C.13.1 Testing vs. Evaluation vs. User Acceptance
| Dimension | Core Question | Evidence Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Testing** | Does the software behave as specified? | Automated unit/integration tests, edge cases, defect registers. |
| **Evaluation** | Is the technical solution effective? | Baseline comparisons, latency/accuracy metrics, ablation studies. |
| **User Acceptance** | Will the target community adopt it? | UAT with target users, task-completion times, recorded usability trials. |

*Proxy Rule:* If target users cannot be engaged due to safety/clinical reasons, domain experts or proxies must be explicitly declared at proposal defense.

### C.14 External Components and Student Ownership

#### Dependency Classification
- **Support:** Standard libraries, utility frameworks, boilerplate scaffolding, UI component sets. *Requirement: Disclose licenses.*
- **Core-Assist:** Pre-trained models, hosted cloud services, ML backbones. *Requirement: Permitted only if students build significant custom pipelines, fine-tuning, error handling, or validation around it.*
- **Core-Replacement:** External API or off-the-shelf system completely solves the CCP. *Status: Unacceptable. Marks capped or project rejected.*

#### Standard of Understanding for Claimed Code
For any CCP-relevant component, the owning student must be able to:
1. **Explain** the underlying mechanism and data flow without reading code.
2. **Justify** the architectural configuration against rejected alternatives.
3. **Predict** exactly what breaks when inputs, scale, or parameters shift.
4. **Modify** the implementation live under examiner questioning.

### C.15 Common Weak Idea Patterns
- **Routine Portal / CRUD Application:** High screen count, zero algorithmic depth. *Remedy: Target hard optimization, concurrency, or domain trade-offs.*
- **Generic Chatbot / RAG Wrapper:** Simple API forwarding to an LLM provider. *Remedy: Design sophisticated retrieval pipelines, custom chunking, evaluation baselines, and safety guards.*
- **Model Training without Problem or Baseline:** Reporting a loss curve on a public dataset without application context. *Remedy: Measure real-world latency, domain shift, and deployable integration.*
- **Local Copy of an Existing Product:** Rebuilding an existing app with only language changes. *Remedy: Identify localized operational constraints that break standard architectures.*
- **Jupyter Notebook FYP (Stream A):** High accuracy in Python notebook, no deployable system. *Remedy: Engineer into an operable application with deployment pipelines.*
- **Volume-of-Code Project:** Attempting to claim credit simply for writing thousands of lines of routine code. *Remedy: Relocate difficulty to trade-offs, constraints, and empirical evaluation.*

### C.18 Stream A: Starting from Published Research

#### C.18.1 Paper Selection Checklist
- Prefer recent peer-reviewed literature (CVPR, NeurIPS, ICSE, IEEE/ACM journals) over unreviewed preprints.
- Ensure code, data, and compute requirements are reproducible within local hardware budgets.
- Ensure the paper leaves an explicit gap, untested setting, or unaddressed operational constraint.

#### C.18.2 Establishing the Research Delta
- **Formula:**
  > *"Paper X reports result A on dataset B under assumption C. We will reproduce, adapt, or extend it to D, where assumption C does not hold, and evaluate using E."*
- **Valid Deltas:** Transparent reproduction of unverified methods, adaptation to novel resource-constrained settings, edge optimization, empirical benchmarking across models, or operationalizing a raw algorithm into a usable software product.

---

### C.19 Stream B: Starting from a Real-World Problem

#### C.19.1 Problem Validation
- Requires direct stakeholder interviews, observed workflow metrics, and quantification of current failure costs.
- Structural analysis of why off-the-shelf commercial tools fail to address the local environment.

#### C.19.2 Sourcing Genuine Complexity
- Real-world complexity arises from environmental barriers: intermittent connectivity (offline-first sync), compute-constrained hardware, noisy/unstructured local data, multi-stakeholder authority conflicts, and strict data privacy regulations.
- *Test:* If all environmental constraints were removed, would a non-trivial computing challenge remain? If not, those constraints *are* the project.

### C.20 Three-Level CCP Formulation Examples
*(Mapping the Seoul Accord problem tiers: Weak = Well-defined / Technician; Developing = Broadly-defined / Technologist; Strong = Complex Computing Problem / Professional Graduate).*

#### C.20.1 University Timetabling Product (Stream B)
- **Weak:** Develop an automated university timetable system that allocates courses, faculty members, and rooms while avoiding clashes.
- **Developing:** Develop a timetable-generation system that considers faculty availability, room capacities, laboratory requirements, student course clashes, instructor preferences, and preferred teaching hours.
- **Strong:** Develop a timetable-generation and repair system that must satisfy zero faculty, room, and student-batch clashes; laboratory, equipment, accessibility, and restricted-availability constraints; and must balance compact student schedules, fair distribution of undesirable hours, instructor preferences, room utilization, and minimum disruption when late changes make all preferences impossible to satisfy simultaneously.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8)*

#### C.20.2 Institutional Information Assistant (Stream B)
- **Weak:** Develop a RAG-based university chatbot that answers questions from university documents.
- **Developing:** Develop a university assistant that processes policies and handbooks, provides citations, supports multiple user roles, and updates its knowledge base when documents change.
- **Strong:** Develop a university information assistant that must answer from documents differing in authority, effective date, and access level; distinguish current from superseded rules; prevent restricted-information disclosure; identify conflicting or insufficient evidence; and balance answer completeness, response time, and the risk of unsupported responses.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9)*

#### C.20.3 Fraud Investigation Product (Stream B)
- **Weak:** Develop an AI-based system that detects fraudulent transactions and displays alerts on a dashboard.
- **Developing:** Develop a fraud-monitoring product that combines transaction, account, device, and channel history to calculate risk scores and prioritize alerts.
- **Strong:** Develop a fraud-investigation product that must identify coordinated and evolving behavior across accounts, devices, channels, and time while controlling false positives, preserving privacy, producing traceable explanations, prioritizing limited investigator capacity, and meeting near-real-time response constraints.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9)*

#### C.20.4 Coordinated Traffic-Control Product (Stream B)
- **Weak:** Develop a smart traffic system that detects vehicles and changes signal timings.
- **Developing:** Develop a traffic-management system for multiple intersections that considers vehicle volume, pedestrians, and emergency vehicles.
- **Strong:** Develop a coordinated traffic-control product that must preserve pedestrian and intersection safety, prioritize emergency vehicles, balance waiting time across competing traffic streams, prevent local optimization from worsening network congestion, and continue operating under uncertain flows, sensor errors, and communication delays.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8)*

#### C.20.5 Recruitment Decision-Support Product (Stream B)
- **Weak:** Develop an AI-based recruitment platform that ranks CVs against job descriptions.
- **Developing:** Develop a recruitment product that evaluates education, experience, and skills, ranks applicants, and provides explanations to recruiters.
- **Strong:** Develop a recruitment decision-support product that must reason from incomplete and inconsistently presented evidence, balance conflicting job criteria, control unfair exclusion and proxy bias, preserve auditability, support justified human overrides, and communicate uncertainty where candidate evidence is insufficient.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9)*

#### C.20.6 Environmental-Risk Monitoring Product (Stream B)
- **Weak:** Develop an IoT system that displays sensor readings and sends alerts when values cross thresholds.
- **Developing:** Develop an environmental monitoring product that combines readings from multiple sensors, reports trends, and detects abnormal conditions.
- **Strong:** Develop an environmental-risk monitoring product that must distinguish hazardous events from sensor drift, failure, and communication loss; combine heterogeneous observations of different reliability; preserve timely alerts under power and bandwidth limits; and balance missed-event risk against false alarms that cause unnecessary intervention.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9)*

#### C.20.7 Diagnostic-Support Product (Medical Imaging) (Stream A)
- **Weak:** Reproduce a published deep-learning model for medical-image classification.
- **Developing:** Apply and compare published models on a local dataset and improve classification accuracy.
- **Strong:** Develop a diagnostic-support product that must operate on heterogeneous, low-quality, and partially missing field images; recognize distribution shift and uncertain cases; control clinically dangerous false negatives; provide traceable evidence; and operate within the compute and connectivity limits of the intended setting.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9)*

#### C.20.8 Urdu Opinion-Analysis Product (Stream A)
- **Weak:** Fine-tune a published language model for Urdu sentiment analysis.
- **Developing:** Develop an Urdu sentiment model that handles Roman Urdu, code-switching, and spelling variation.
- **Strong:** Develop an Urdu opinion-analysis product that must handle code-switching, Roman Urdu, sarcasm, evolving vocabulary, scarce and inconsistent labels, domain shift, and unequal performance across user groups while providing interpretable evidence for high-impact moderation or decision-support cases.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9)*

#### C.20.9 Federated Learning Product (Stream A)
- **Weak:** Implement federated learning using an existing framework and default aggregation.
- **Developing:** Train a shared model across multiple organizations without exchanging raw data and compare it with centralized training.
- **Strong:** Develop a federated learning product that must maintain useful and fair performance across highly non-identical institutional data, intermittent participation, unequal client resources, communication limits, privacy threats, and unreliable client updates without exposing local data.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8)*

#### C.20.10 Misleading-Chart Review Product (Stream A)
- **Weak:** Implement a published model for detecting misleading charts.
- **Developing:** Detect multiple chart types and classify common misleading visual patterns.
- **Strong:** Develop a chart-review product that must analyze charts extracted from heterogeneous images, PDFs, and dashboards; reason with incomplete metadata and surrounding context; distinguish design error from intentional distortion; control costly false accusations; explain supporting evidence; and route uncertain cases for human review.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9)*

#### C.20.11 Legal Information Product (Stream A)
- **Weak:** Fine-tune a pre-trained language model for legal question answering.
- **Developing:** Develop a legal question-answering system that retrieves jurisdiction-specific documents and provides citations.
- **Strong:** Develop a legal information product that must distinguish binding, advisory, superseded, and jurisdiction-specific sources; handle conflicting authorities and changing law; prevent restricted-document disclosure; identify insufficient evidence; and balance answer usefulness with the consequences of unsupported guidance.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9)*

#### C.20.12 Operational Intrusion-Analysis Product (Stream A)
- **Weak:** Reproduce a published intrusion-detection model on a public benchmark dataset.
- **Developing:** Compare several intrusion-detection models and improve detection accuracy on local network data.
- **Strong:** Develop an operational intrusion-analysis product that must detect evolving and low-frequency attacks from imbalanced, partially labelled, and changing traffic; control alert overload; preserve response time; remain robust to adversarial manipulation; provide evidence suitable for investigation; and adapt without forgetting previously detected behaviors.  
*(Exhibits Seoul Accord Characteristics: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9)*
